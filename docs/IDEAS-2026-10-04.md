### Before this goes live

1. **Apply migrations 0026 to 0035 to the hosted Supabase project** (0026 to 0029 were already pending on `main`).
2. **ID checks are on by default.** Existing customers are all unverified, so providers won't be able to accept *self-drive* requests until those customers verify (chauffeur rentals are unaffected). Either staff the ID queue before release, or set `REQUIRE_CUSTOMER_VERIFICATION=false` and switch it on later.
3. **Mobile money:** open a Notch Pay business account, set `NOTCHPAY_PUBLIC_KEY` and `NOTCHPAY_WEBHOOK_HASH` on the API, and point their webhook at `<API>/api/payments/webhook/notchpay`. Their docs don't publish the webhook payload shape, so the adapter accepts the common field names; test one real sandbox payment end to end before trusting it.
4. **Deposit before handover** switches on by itself once Stripe or Notch Pay is configured. Set `REQUIRE_DEPOSIT_BEFORE_HANDOVER=false` if you want the old behaviour while deposits are still handled by hand.
5. **Chat translation** needs `DEEPL_API_KEY` (the same key reviews use).
6. **Map tiles:** OpenStreetMap's own tiles are fine for testing but their policy asks apps to use a provider. Set `VITE_MAP_TILE_URL` (e.g. MapTiler) and add its host to `img-src` in `apps/web/vercel.json`.
7. **Providers need to drop their pin** (dashboard, "Your location and delivery area") before they show up on the map or in "near me".
8. **Tracking is web-only:** the driver's phone stops sharing if the page closes. A native wrapper (e.g. Capacitor) is the fix if this becomes core.

## 1. Diaspora payment and local-friendly payment

**Today.** `apps/api/src/payments/provider.ts` defines `PaymentProviderAdapter`. Two implementations: `ManualPaymentProvider` (live, takes no money) and `StripeCardProvider` (written and tested, switched on by `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` + `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`). Deposit is 15% of total (`DEPOSIT_RATE`). There is no commission or platform fee anywhere in the code.

**Recommendation.**
- **Diaspora (card):** Stripe is already built. Blocker is Gate A (can a UK entity hold Cameroonian deposits). Stripe cannot onboard a Cameroonian merchant, so this goes through the UK entity. Turn on Apple Pay / Google Pay in Checkout too, almost free once Stripe is live.
- **Local (mobile money):** add a second adapter for an aggregator covering both MTN MoMo and Orange Money in XAF. Candidates to compare: **Campay** and **Notch Pay** (Cameroon-native), **CinetPay**, **Flutterwave**, **pawaPay**. Pick on payout speed to vendors, fees, and whether they support refunds by API.
- **Checkout UX:** let the customer pick the method, defaulting by signals (non-Cameroon phone code or currency switcher set to GBP/EUR → card; +237 → mobile money). The adapter interface needs one small change: choose the provider per payment instead of one global env var.
- **"Pay for someone else":** the diaspora use case is a person abroad paying while family drives. Make the payer and the driver separate fields on the booking. This also matters for #9 (verify the driver, not the payer).

**Open questions.** Gate A answer. Does Karu want to take a commission on top of the deposit, and how is the remaining 85% paid (cash to vendor at handover, or through Karu)? If through Karu, the vendor payout side needs building too.

## 2. Translation button for booking comments (Instagram style)

**Today.** `apps/api/src/reviews/translation.ts` already has `DeepLProvider` + `NullTranslationProvider`, and reviews already record the author's language (migration `0024`). Booking chat (`BookingChat.tsx`, `messages.service.ts`) has none of this.

**Recommendation.**
- Add `language` to `booking_messages` (the author's locale at send time).
- `POST /bookings/:id/messages/:msgId/translate` returns the translation, cached on the row (`translations jsonb`, keyed by target language) so the same message is never paid for twice.
- In the bubble: a small grey "See translation" link only when the message language differs from the reader's locale; tap swaps the text in place with "See original". That is exactly the Instagram pattern.
- Translate the **redacted** text, never the raw input, so translation cannot leak a hidden phone number.
- Free DeepL tier (500k chars/month) covers this easily at current volume.

## 3. Uber-style tracking of the vehicle

Two very different things hide under this:

| Option | What it tracks | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Delivery leg (recommended first)** | The driver bringing the car to the customer / airport | Driver's phone shares location via the browser Geolocation API, pushed over Supabase Realtime, shown on a map with ETA | Only active between "on the way" and handover. Fits #4 and #8. |
| **B. Whole rental** | The car itself for the full trip | A GPS tracker in the car (many Douala fleets already have one), read through something like Traccar | Anti-theft value for vendors. Big privacy question for customers. |

**Caveat that shapes the plan:** Karu is a web app. iOS and Android stop browser geolocation when the screen locks, so option A only works if the driver keeps the page open. Reliable background tracking needs a native app (or a thin wrapper like Capacitor). Worth deciding before building.

**Privacy:** location only during the delivery leg, never stored beyond the booking, shown only to that booking's customer. Write this down as policy.

## 4. Deliver to address: best way to charge, and positive for the customer

**Today.** One `delivery_fee_xaf` per vendor, regardless of distance. Customer types a free-text address.

**Problems.** A flat fee is unfair both ways (Akwa vs. far out of town), and typed addresses in Douala / Yaoundé are often landmark-based ("behind the pharmacy"), so the driver still has to phone.

**Recommendation.**
- **Zones, not per-km.** Vendor sets 2 or 3 rings from their base (e.g. within 5 km, 5 to 15 km, beyond: on request). Easier to understand than a per-km meter and no surprise total.
- **Make it feel like a gift:** "Free delivery on rentals of 3 days or more" (vendor-configurable threshold). On longer rentals the fee is small next to the rental, and the vendor can fold it into the daily rate. Show it as a struck-through fee with "Free" next to it.
- **Collection on return** offered with delivery as a pair, priced once, so the customer doesn't hit a second surprise fee at the end.
- **Pin on a map** instead of (or as well as) a typed address, plus a "landmark / directions" note field. Needs the coordinates work in #8.
- Keep the fee frozen on the booking at request time (already done).

## 5. Handover and return code: nicer UI

**Today.** `BookingDetailScreen.tsx:182` renders the code as a line of text only for `confirmed` bookings. Vendor enters it in a plain input.

**Recommendation.**
- **Customer:** a ticket-style card. Four large digits in separate boxes, a QR code underneath (encodes booking ref + code) that the vendor can scan instead of typing, and a hint line ("Show this only when you are with the car").
- **Trip strip:** Confirmed → Handover → On the road → Returned, with the current step highlighted. The return code appears automatically once the trip starts.
- **Vendor:** OTP-style four-box input with auto-advance and paste support, plus "Scan code" using the camera.
- Small confetti or check animation on success (ties into #6).
- Also fix the security gap in section 10 while touching this.

## 6. Enable transitions

**Today.** Motion exists in only `layout.css`, `ui.tsx`, `PhotoCarousel.tsx` and `AssistantPanel.tsx`.

**Recommendation.** The `transitions-agent` skill is available in this environment and is built for exactly this: scan for missing or janky motion and fix it with consistent recipes. Priorities: page/route changes (View Transitions API), modal and panel open/close, button press states, skeleton-to-content fades, list items appearing. Everything wrapped in `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 7. Adaptive pricing

This could mean two things, both worth having:

**a. Demand-based (dynamic) prices.**
- **Phase 1, seasonal rules (S to M):** vendor or admin sets date-range multipliers, e.g. +20% from 15 Dec to 5 Jan, +15% in July and August (diaspora season). Applied inside `quoteBooking` in `packages/shared` so the quote and the server agree, and frozen on the booking like everything else.
- **Phase 2, smart suggestions (M):** suggest a price per date based on lead time, how booked the vendor's fleet is, and city-wide demand. Vendor stays in control with a floor and ceiling, Airbnb "Smart Pricing" style. Don't auto-change prices without consent; vendors will distrust it.
- Also worth adding: **long-rental discounts** are already there (weekly/monthly rates); **last-minute** and **early-bird** discounts are cheap extensions.

**b. Price shown in the visitor's own currency.** A diaspora customer in London should see £ first with XAF alongside. `CurrencySwitcher.tsx` and the display-conversion helper already exist; auto-select the currency from the browser locale. Stripe also has a feature literally called *Adaptive Pricing* that charges the customer in their local currency at checkout, which pairs with #1.

## 8. Location maps: find the closest vendor

**Today.** `vendors.city` and `vehicles.city` are an enum, `vehicles.pickup_locations` is free text, `vendors.address` is free text. No coordinates anywhere. The CSP in `apps/web/vercel.json` would also block any map tiles.

**Recommendation.**
- **Data:** add `lat`/`lng` (or a PostGIS `geography(Point)`) to vendors and to each pickup point (pickup points become their own table rather than a text array).
- **Onboarding:** vendor drops a pin on a map; admin sees it during verification.
- **Customer:** "Near me" button (browser geolocation, asked only on click), results sorted by distance, map view with price pins like Airbnb, list/map toggle on `/search`.
- **Map stack:** MapLibre GL with free OpenStreetMap-based tiles (OpenFreeMap or MapTiler free tier) rather than Google Maps, which gets expensive. OSM coverage of central Douala and Yaoundé is reasonable; check outer districts.
- **Privacy:** show an approximate circle for home-based vendors until a booking is confirmed, exact pin after.
- Unlocks #3 and #4.

## 9. ID verification of the customer before handover

**Today.** Vendors upload RCCM / national ID / passport and admin reviews them. Customers have no verification at all: anyone with an email can book a self-drive car. Vendors only see the customer's short name.

**Recommendation, tiered:**
1. **Upload + admin review (M, no new vendor):** customer uploads driving licence (front/back), CNI or passport, and a selfie. Reuse the vendor document flow, storage bucket rules and admin queue. Required before a **self-drive** booking can be confirmed; lighter or none for with-driver bookings.
2. **Check at handover (S, on top of 1):** the vendor's handover screen shows the customer's verified name and selfie, so the vendor matches the face and the physical licence before entering the code. This is the real point of the feature.
3. **Automated KYC (L, later):** **Smile ID** has the strongest coverage of African IDs including Cameroon; Veriff, Onfido and Didit are alternatives. Swap in behind an adapter like payments and translation once volume justifies the per-check cost.

**Also:** licence expiry date and minimum age / years licensed rules per vehicle. Verify the *driver*, who may not be the payer (see #1). Check obligations under Cameroon's personal data protection law before storing ID scans; keep them in a private bucket with short-lived signed URLs and a retention period.

## 10. Loopholes: ways customers or vendors could cheat Karu

Found by reading the code. Ordered by how much they could cost.

### High

1. **Vendor phone handed over before any money moves.** `getDetailForUser` in `bookings.service.ts` reveals the vendor's `contact_phone` once a booking is `confirmed`. Because confirmation needs no deposit (see 2), a customer can request, get confirmed, take the number, cancel, and deal with the vendor directly for free, cutting Karu out of this rental and every future one. The chat redaction (`messages/redact.ts`) can't help once this has happened. **Fix:** reveal the phone only once the deposit is `held` (or on the handover day), or route calls through Karu.
2. **No money changes hands before confirmation.** With the manual provider, a customer can request and get confirmed with nothing paid, then no-show. Vendors learn the platform doesn't protect them. **Fix:** a booking only becomes `confirmed` once the deposit is `held` (needs #1).
3. **Free vendor cancellation of confirmed bookings.** A vendor can cancel a confirmed booking at any time with no penalty, e.g. after finding a better or off-platform rental. **Fix:** cancellation policy for both sides: refund rules for the customer, a strike count / penalty / ranking hit for the vendor, and admin visibility.
4. **Fake reviews via self-booking.** Nothing stops a vendor creating a customer account and booking their own car. They hold both codes, so they can drive it to `completed` and leave themselves five stars. **Fix:** block bookings where customer and vendor share a phone, email domain pattern or device; once #9 is in, require a verified customer to review; flag accounts whose only bookings are with one vendor.

### Medium

5. **Handover code can be guessed.** It is four digits (`generateHandoverCode`, 9,000 options) and the transition endpoint allows 20 tries a minute, so a vendor could mark a trip started or finished without the customer present. Uses `Math.random`. **Fix:** lock the booking after 5 wrong codes and alert admin; use `crypto.randomInt`; keep codes short for usability.
6. **Return code before inspection.** If the customer reads the return code out as they hand the keys over, the booking completes before the vendor checks for damage, and there is no record of the car's condition at either end. **Fix:** handover and return photo checklist (fuel, mileage, four sides) captured in-app by both parties; vendor has a short window after return to raise a damage claim.
7. **Flat delivery fee abuse.** A customer can enter an address 60 km away for the same fee as next door. Fixed by #4.
8. **Chat redaction is easy to beat** (spelled-out numbers, splitting across messages, a photo of a business card). Accept it as a guardrail; add admin flags on messages mentioning "WhatsApp", "call me", "cash", and track cancel-after-chat patterns.

### Lower / to check

9. **Extending a trip off-platform:** the customer keeps the car extra days and pays the vendor cash. Offer an in-app "extend trip" so it stays easy to do honestly.
10. **Duplicate accounts after a ban:** email normalisation exists (REQ-7), but phone and, later, verified ID should be unique per active account.
11. **Vendor listing a car they don't own or that has expired papers:** document expiry alerts exist (REQ-12); consider auto-pausing the car on expiry and matching the carte grise plate to the listed plate.

## 11. UI colour palette config (like Monkeytype)

**Today.** `apps/web/src/ds/tokens.css` defines everything as CSS variables and the components use them (363 `var(--...)` references, only about 17 hardcoded colours to clean up). No dark mode.

**Recommendation.**
- Define themes as `[data-theme="..."]` blocks that override the semantic tokens (`--bg-page`, `--surface-card`, `--brand`, `--text-primary`, ...), not the raw palette.
- Start with a small curated set rather than Monkeytype's hundreds: **Karu (default)**, **Dark**, **Light / high contrast**, maybe one or two fun ones. Brand recognition matters more for a marketplace than for a typing site.
- Picker in Profile with live preview swatches; saved to `localStorage` for guests and to `profiles` for signed-in users; follow the OS dark-mode setting by default.
- Check contrast (WCAG AA) for every theme, especially gold text on cream.
